Software is cognitive infrastructure

Modern software is no longer just a collection of features. It shapes how developers think, decide, and work.

  • reduces working memory load
  • clarifies decisions
  • preserves context

Frosted//Logic exists to push that balance toward clarity. We build systems that help developers focus on solving problems — not fighting their tools.

Three kinds of systems

Our work falls into three complementary categories.

Microtools

Deterministic utilities that solve narrowly scoped developer problems. They are: focused, fast, reliable, often offline-friendly, built to ship quickly and stay useful for years.

Microtools remove friction at the tactical level.

Instruments

Intelligence systems that reveal where leverage exists in the development process. They: analyze signals, synthesize information, surface patterns or risks, guide higher-level decisions.

Instruments improve how software is built at the strategic level.

Interactive Systems

Exploratory software experiences that apply Frosted//Logic principles in interactive or simulated environments. These systems:

  • model decision-making under constraints
  • explore feedback loops and progression
  • test ideas about learning, systems thinking, or developer intuition
  • prioritize clarity of mechanics over spectacle

They serve as experimental spaces where ideas about systems, feedback, and cognition can be explored differently than in traditional tools.

Microtools solve problems. Instruments discover which problems are worth solving. Interactive systems explore how those solutions behave in practice.

How We Design Software

  • Determinism where possible
  • Local-first by default

Development is already hard enough

Most developer friction doesn’t come from algorithms or languages. It comes from unclear workflows, noisy tools, lost context, avoidable mistakes. Small improvements in clarity compound over time. Frosted//Logic focuses on those leverage points — the places where a well-designed system can quietly save hours, days, or entire failed projects.
